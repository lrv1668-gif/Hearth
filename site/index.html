A living picture frame that knows your home.

Hearth gathers the weather, your day's tasks and plans, the moon, the season and a little art into one quiet display — made for a color e-paper frame on the wall, and just as at home in a browser.

The idea

Most home dashboards are control panels. Hearth is designed to recede.

Like a fireplace, it's always on and always present, but it earns its place in the room by being calm and good to look at — not by asking for your attention.

1

Paper, not a screen

E-paper reflects light instead of emitting it. No glow in a dark room, no power draw to hold an image — it looks like a print hanging on the wall.

2

Snapshots, not feeds

The frame is designed for a few deliberate refreshes, not a live feed, so it shows only things that stay true in between — no ticking clocks, live departures or now-playing.

3

Yours, at home

Everything runs on a box on your own network and stores its state there. Weather, calendar and music hookups are optional extras, not requirements.

On the wall

Six ways to be in the room.

Most of the time the frame is a full-bleed picture or a structured morning edition — Ambient by default, Daily when you want the day laid out. Four quieter modes fill in around them, and a schedule you set decides which one is up.

Ambient · the default

The art is the display.

A full-frame photograph — from Unsplash by season and category, or your own uploads — with a small card in one corner: the date, the temperature, a line about the weather, and credit for the photographer. Information stays peripheral.

Daily · the morning edition

A morning edition for your household.

A masthead with the date, season and today's sun path; two columns of widgets you arrange yourself; a five-day forecast along the bottom. Type sized to read from across the room — in full color, or a crisp black-and-white broadsheet.

Plus Photo: pin one of your own uploads, or an album of them, and it stays up until you switch it off. Daily on weekday mornings and Night after ten, say, with Ambient the rest of the time — or pick one by hand from Settings.

Frame themes

Thirteen palettes, mixed from six inks.

The Spectra 6 panel prints with black, white, red, yellow, blue and green. Every frame theme is built only from those measured inks and a few approved two-ink mixes, so what you pick on screen is what the panel can actually show.

Also: Gallery, Harbor, Postbox, Ember and Lantern; a black-and-white mode for every theme; nine typefaces; a portrait layout for a frame hung on its side; and any panel size.

Your layout

Pick what's worth a glance.

Arrange the Daily columns with the same drag-and-drop editor the web dashboard uses: Today's Arc, Today, Coming Up, Week Ahead, Countdowns, Almanac, Moon Phase, Daily Quote and Birds Nearby.

Each widget trims itself to the space it's given, so a short column stays tidy and a long one fills in — no overflowing lists, no tiny print.

Any wall

Landscape or portrait, any size.

Hang the frame whichever way suits the wall. The Daily layout reflows for a tall panel instead of just shrinking.

Using a different panel? Set its resolution in Settings → Frame, and the type and spacing scale to match.

Settings → Frame

See it the way the panel will.

The frame's settings live on the host, so every phone and browser in the house edits the same frame. The Mode card shows what's up now, takes a manual override for an hour or until you change it back, and holds the schedule.

Beside it, the preview switches between the live page and the host's own capture, dithered down to the six inks — with a Paper view that mimics e-paper's greyer, softer colors.

In the browser

The same home, on every screen.

The web dashboard is where you add tasks, set up integrations and arrange the frame — and it makes a fine always-on display for a spare tablet, too.

Calendar

Tasks and plans in one place.

Hearth keeps its own tasks — one-offs, repeating chores, countdowns to the dates you're looking forward to — and lays them out next to your Google Calendar events.

Tick something off on your phone and every screen in the house agrees.

Phone

The frame's remote control.

On a phone the dashboard becomes a single column under a one-line date and weather header, with a tab bar along the bottom — the quickest way to add a task, switch the frame's mode or check what's coming up.

Settings opens as a list of sections, dialogs slide up as sheets, and the frame preview waits behind a button until you want it.

How it fits together

A thin frame, a capable host.

The frame does almost nothing: fetch a finished picture, show it, listen for a button. Everything else happens on a small server somewhere on your network.

Your phone & browser

Add tasks, connect accounts, choose themes and lay out the frame.

The host

A Raspberry Pi 4/5 or mini PC running Docker Compose: a SvelteKit app and eleven small .NET services behind Caddy, each with its own SQLite file, plus a headless-Chromium renderer that captures and dithers the frame.
